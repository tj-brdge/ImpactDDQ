ANALYST RESEARCH NOTES — MICROFINANCE PLUS
Prepared by: Research Team, Impact Capital Partners
Date: November 2023

COMPANY OVERVIEW

MicroFinance Plus is a mobile-first lending platform headquartered in Lagos, Nigeria, serving smallholder farmers and micro-entrepreneurs across West Africa. Founded in 2018 by Chidi Eze and Fatima Bello, the company has grown rapidly to serve over 500,000 active borrowers with a remarkable 97% repayment rate.

The platform operates primarily through USSD and mobile app interfaces, making it accessible even on feature phones. Loan sizes range from $50 to $5,000 with terms of 3-12 months.

KEY PERSONNEL NOTES

Chidi Eze (CEO) — Previously led microfinance operations at Access Bank Nigeria. Strong relationships with Central Bank of Nigeria regulatory team. Stanford MBA.

Fatima Bello (COO) — Background in agricultural extension services with the Nigerian Ministry of Agriculture. Deep understanding of smallholder farmer economics. Named to Forbes Africa 30 Under 30 in 2020.

Oluwaseun Adeyemi (CTO) — Former Andela fellow, built the core lending algorithm. The credit scoring model uses alternative data (mobile money transactions, crop cycle patterns, social network analysis).

Board includes representatives from Omidyar Network, Mastercard Foundation, and a former Governor of the Central Bank of Nigeria.

FINANCIAL ANALYSIS

Revenue Breakdown (2023):
- Interest income: $8.2M (average APR 24%)
- Processing fees: $1.8M
- Insurance premiums (crop insurance): $0.9M
- Total revenue: $10.9M

Growth trajectory:
- 2020: $1.2M revenue, 50,000 borrowers
- 2021: $3.4M revenue, 150,000 borrowers
- 2022: $6.8M revenue, 320,000 borrowers
- 2023: $10.9M revenue, 500,000 borrowers

Operating costs: $8.5M (78% cost-to-income ratio)
Net margin: 15% (improving from 5% in 2022)

Total funding: $35M across Seed ($2M), Series A ($12M), Series B ($21M)
Series B led by Partech Africa at $85M valuation.

IMPACT ASSESSMENT

The company's impact thesis centers on financial inclusion for underserved populations in Lagos, Abuja, Kano, and increasingly rural areas of Nigeria, Ghana, and Senegal.

Key impact metrics:
- 500,000+ active borrowers (65% women, 70% rural)
- Average income increase of 40% for borrowers after 2 loan cycles
- 180,000 smallholder farmers accessing credit for the first time
- Crop insurance payouts: $2.1M disbursed in 2023 (covering 45,000 farmers)
- Financial literacy training completed by 280,000 users

Framework: Custom impact measurement framework aligned with IRIS+ and the Universal Standards for Social Performance Management.

Verification: Annual social audit by MicroFinanza Rating (certified by CGAP).

MARKET POSITION

Nigeria alone has 40M+ unbanked adults. West Africa represents a $25B credit gap for MSMEs.

Key competitors:
- FairMoney (Nigeria) — $42M raised, consumer lending focus
- Carbon (Nigeria) — Digital banking, less agriculture focus
- Moniepoint — Business payments, expanding to lending

MicroFinance Plus differentiates through agricultural specialization, the crop insurance bundle, and USSD accessibility for rural users.

RISK ASSESSMENT

1. Regulatory: Central Bank of Nigeria tightening digital lending regulations. New licensing requirements expected 2024.
2. Currency: Naira volatility (NGN depreciated 40% in 2023). Dollar-denominated investors face FX exposure.
3. Concentration: 80% of portfolio in Nigeria. Ghana and Senegal still early.
4. Credit risk: 97% repayment impressive but untested through economic downturn.
5. Technology: Core banking system migration planned for Q1 2024 — execution risk.
6. Political: Nigerian elections and policy uncertainty.

ESG NOTES

Environmental: Paperless operations, solar-powered branch offices in Lagos and Abuja
Social: Strong gender lens (65% women borrowers), living wage commitment, no aggressive collection practices
Governance: Independent audit committee, published responsible lending principles
Red flags: None identified

RECOMMENDATION

Strong candidate for portfolio inclusion. The combination of high growth, improving unit economics, clear impact metrics, and experienced management team makes this an attractive Series B+ opportunity. Key monitoring items: Naira exposure, regulatory developments, and credit performance through economic cycles.
