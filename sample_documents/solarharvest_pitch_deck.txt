EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SolarHarvest Energy is a distributed solar microgrid company providing affordable, clean electricity to off-grid communities across East Africa. Founded in 2019 by Dr. Amina Okafor and James Mwangi in Nairobi, Kenya, the company has grown to operate 47 microgrids serving over 120,000 households.

Our mission: universal energy access through decentralized solar infrastructure that is affordable, reliable, and community-owned.

FOUNDING STORY

Dr. Amina Okafor, a renewable energy engineer from the University of Cape Town, witnessed firsthand the energy poverty affecting rural Kenyan communities while conducting field research in 2017. Partnering with James Mwangi, a veteran of the mobile money industry in Nairobi, they developed a pay-as-you-go solar microgrid model that leverages M-Pesa for seamless payments.

The company was incorporated in Nairobi in 2019 and deployed its first microgrid in Turkana County within six months.

IMPACT THESIS

Target Beneficiaries: Rural and peri-urban households in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda currently relying on kerosene or diesel for lighting and power.

Theory of Change: By providing affordable solar electricity ($0.15/kWh vs. $0.80+/kWh for kerosene equivalent), we enable households to save money, children to study after dark, and local businesses to extend operating hours. Each microgrid creates 5-8 local jobs for maintenance and operations.

SDG Alignment: SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy), SDG 13 (Climate Action), SDG 8 (Decent Work), SDG 1 (No Poverty)

BUSINESS MODEL

Revenue Model: Pay-as-you-go tariff ($0.15/kWh), connection fees ($25 one-time), and commercial/institutional contracts (schools, health clinics) at premium rates.

Unit Economics:
- Average microgrid serves 2,500 households
- Installation cost: $180,000 per microgrid
- Monthly revenue per microgrid: $12,500
- Payback period: 18 months
- Gross margin: 62%

Customer Segments: Residential (75%), Commercial/SME (15%), Institutional (10%)

MARKET OPPORTUNITY

Total Addressable Market: 600 million people in Sub-Saharan Africa lack electricity access. East Africa alone represents a $14B annual opportunity for off-grid energy.

Market Growth: Off-grid solar market growing at 25% CAGR in East Africa.

Regulatory Tailwinds: Kenya's Energy Act 2019 provides tax incentives for mini-grid operators. Tanzania's Rural Electrification Agency offers co-investment programs.
